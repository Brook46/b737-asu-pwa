The parts

Measure first

The models use datasheet and listing sizes, not boards in hand. Measure these with calipers when the parts arrive and set them at the top of the .scad file before printing. Then run hardware/enclosure/build.sh, which needs OpenSCAD, to regenerate the STLs.

Print

  • PETG, not PLA: a case in the sun on a cockpit, or in a car, softens PLA.
  • Light colour, white or light grey. A dark case in the sun heats the air the sensors read. Print the shield in white.
  • 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 30 % infill. The walls are thin, so most parts come out nearly solid.
  • Tray, lids, plate and remote parts print flat side down; caps print upright. The shield prints cap down. None need supports: the shield's louvres are 45° and the plate's tunnel is a short bridge.

Buy beside the Seeed order

Main case

  • 4 × M2 heat-set inserts (3.2 mm hole) and 4 × M2 × 6 countersunk screws. Plain M2 self-tapping screws also work in PETG.
  • 2 × M2 × 4 screws for the strap plate, driven from inside the case.
  • Open-cell foam, a 10 × 10 mm piece, for the vent pocket.
  • Slide switch SS12D00 (or similar, ≥ 0.5 A) for the battery lead.
  • Velcro (cockpit) or 25 mm webbing (riser strap plate).
  • A cord to tie the case to the harness. A dropped instrument is gone.

Screen version

  • Adafruit 4694, SHARP Memory Display Breakout 2.7" 400×240. It runs on 3 V or 5 V with 3 V logic; check stock, as some shops list it as out of stock.
  • Clear polycarbonate or acrylic sheet, 1 mm, cut to cover the window. It keeps rain off the glass.

Remote

  • Your XIAO ESP32-C6.
  • 3 × 12 × 12 mm tactile switches (7.3 mm with stem) and a perfboard, about 54 × 26 mm.
  • LiPo 402030, about 250 mAh, with a protection board.
  • Grove – Temperature & Humidity Sensor (SHT40) and a 30–50 cm Grove cable.
  • 4 × M2 × 6 self-tapping screws (lid) and 4 × M2 × 4 (perfboard).
  • 20 mm Velcro strap, about 25 cm, around the brake toggle.

Build the main case

  1. Print the tray and a lid. Press the four M2 inserts into the corner bosses with a soldering iron at about 220 °C, square to the boss.
  2. Test-fit the lid before anything goes in: the skirt should slide in without force, and the four screws should pull it flat.
  3. Push the foam into the vent pocket behind the 3 mm hole. It should touch the wall but not be squashed flat.
  4. Battery into its bay on a piece of double-sided foam tape, with its lead toward the switch end. Kapton over the battery's top face.
  5. BME280 flat beside the battery, its sensor end toward the vent. LSM6DS3 on foam tape on top of the battery; any orientation works, since the unit learns "up" at power-on.
  6. XIAO + Wio-SX1262 stack into the cradle at the far end. Check the USB-C plug goes in through the hole before you tape anything.
  7. L76K into its guides beside the XIAO. The slide switch goes into the end-wall window above it, with a drop of glue from inside.
  8. In the lid: GPS patch into its frame, ceramic side against the lid (facing the sky). Buzzer in its frame over the hole ring. Tape the LoRa antenna at least 10 mm from the patch, and the Bluetooth antenna to a tray wall away from the battery.
  9. Wire per the assembly guide, then close the lid without pinching a wire. Power on still, and check health on the console. Close for good only when everything reports.

Mounting

On the cockpit, landscape

Velcro on the flat back. The lid faces up and the GPS patch sees the sky. This suits the headless unit (sound only) and the screen in landscape.

On a riser or shoulder strap, portrait

Screw the strap plate on with its tunnel running along the case's long side, then thread the webbing. The screen sits upright and readable at a glance, and the buzzer is near your ear. Set the instrument to portrait.

Wherever it goes

  • The lid, and the GPS patch, faces the sky as much as possible.
  • Tie it to the harness with a cord.
  • Keep the vent end out of the airflow's direct blast. Foam handles gusts, but the vario is happiest at a side or rear vent.
  • Power on with the unit still, mounted or on the ground.

The remote and its sensor

  1. Solder the three switches to the perfboard, spaced 18 mm apart to match the lid holes. Wire each switch between a free GPIO and GND; the inputs use their internal pull-ups. Check the C6's boot (strapping) pins before choosing.
  2. Battery on the floor, XIAO in its cradle with USB-C at the end hole, then perfboard on its four posts. The switch tops should sit about 1 mm under the lid.
  3. Drop the caps into the lid from inside: flat, domed, flat. Screw the lid on and check each button clicks once per press without sticking.
  4. SHT40: Grove cable out through the end hole (a knot inside for strain relief), into the shield, board hanging from the cap with a cable tie through the two slots.
  5. Strap the remote to the brake toggle through both end tabs. Hang the shield, open end down, 10–20 cm up the brake line or on the riser, away from your hand and out of the sun.

The screen version

Wiring for the Adafruit 4694: VIN → 3V3, GND → GND, CLK → D8, DI → D10. These share the SPI bus with the LoRa radio. CS → D1, which leaves D3 free for a battery-voltage divider. The Sharp panel's chip select is active high, unlike the radio's, and the radio's select must stay off while the screen is written. The firmware's screen driver isn't written yet; the screens are, and run in the simulator.

Stack from the outside in: lid, clear cover sheet, the screen's glass, its board on the four posts. A thin bead of clear silicone around the cover sheet keeps water out of the window.

Before the first flight

  • Lid closed, unit on the table: health shows baro, IMU and GNSS, and show baro barely moves when you breathe near the vent.
  • Outside, clear sky: GNSS fixes within a minute or two with the lid on. If it doesn't, check the patch faces up and is clear of the board and battery.
  • Buzzer test (beep stay) at the mounting spot, with the wind noise of a car window open. Turn the volume up if you have to strain.
  • Shake test: nothing rattles, no wire is pinched under the lid.
  • Tether tied.
