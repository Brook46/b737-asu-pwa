Build notes

  1. Antennas go on the lid, apart from each other. The GNSS patch needs open sky above it and is easily swamped by the ESP32 and the LoRa radio, so it sits over the GNSS board, as far as possible from the XIAO. Keep the LoRa flex antenna at least 10 mm from the patch, and keep both clear of the battery.
  2. Check the antenna band. FANET in Israel runs at 918.5 MHz. Pick a LoRa antenna rated for the 868/915 band, not a narrow 868-only one.
  3. The vent matters. The BMP390 needs still air at outside pressure. A 3 mm hole with open-cell foam behind it lets pressure through but blocks gusts, sun on the sensor and rain. Without a vent, the closed box reads a slow, smoothed pressure. Keep the barometer clear of the ESP32's warm shield.
  4. Add a power switch. It's not in the parts list yet: a small slide switch in the battery's + lead, before the XIAO's BAT pads. Without one, the unit is always on and drains the LiPo in the bag.
  5. Piezo on the lid, behind holes. Glue it over a ring of 1.6 mm holes. Put the 1 kΩ resistor across it on the driver board, or it goes quiet after the first note.
  6. Power on with the unit still. The IMU learns which way is up at boot. Mounting angle doesn't matter after that.
  7. Check the stack heights against your boards. The 26 mm height assumes the Wio-SX1262 kit sits about 3.5 mm under the XIAO, and the XIAO's shield is the tallest part under the lid.
