Assembly guide

How to wire, solder and power the headless paragliding instrument (no screen) from the Seeed parts you ordered, and fit it into its printed case. Work in this order, checking each step before moving on: a working, tested board first, then the case.

What you need on the bench

Tools

  • Temperature-controlled iron (a Pinecil or TS101 is ideal and cheap), with a small conical or 1–1.6 mm chisel tip.
  • Solder: 0.5–0.6 mm rosin-core. 63/37 leaded flows easiest at 330–350 °C; lead-free needs 360–380 °C.
  • Flux pen (no-clean), desoldering wick, brass tip cleaner.
  • Multimeter with a continuity beep. You'll use it more than the iron.
  • Fine wire strippers (26–30 AWG), flush cutters, tweezers.
  • Something to hold work: helping hands, or Blu-Tack on a tile.
  • Magnifier or phone camera zoom, to inspect joints.
  • Heat-shrink tubing (1.5 and 3 mm), Kapton tape, isopropyl alcohol and an old toothbrush.
  • Ventilation (a fan pulling fumes away) and safety glasses.

Buy locally

  • LiPo, 1 cell, 3.7 V, with a protection board. Size it from the battery section below: 500 mAh is about 4–6 h of flying, 1000 mAh about 8–12 h. It must come with a 2-pin JST-PH 2.0 plug, plus a matching socket lead for the XIAO side.
  • Small slide switch (SPDT, 2–3 mm pitch), rated ≥ 0.5 A.
  • Stripboard (copper strips, "Veroboard"): a 4-strip piece, 7 holes long (about 10 × 18 mm), for the distribution board.
  • 2 × 10 kΩ resistors, ¼ W or 0603, for the L76K pull-ups.
  • Silicone wire, 26–28 AWG, red and black, for the battery leads.
  • Optional: a USB power meter, to measure what the unit really draws.
  • For the case: the printed tray and headless lid (PETG, light colour), 4 × M2 heat-set inserts, 4 × M2 × 6 countersunk screws, a 10 × 10 mm piece of open-cell foam, double-sided foam tape, and a cord to tie it to the harness. Strap plate and its 2 × M2 × 4 screws if it goes on a riser. Details in the case and remote guide.

Soldering: the five-minute version

Practise first. Before you touch the XIAO, solder 20 wires to a scrap of the stripboard. That's when your joints start looking right.

  1. Tin the tip. Melt a little solder on the clean hot tip. A shiny tip moves heat; a dull one doesn't.
  2. Tin the wire. Strip 2 mm, twist the strands, touch with flux, then iron and solder until the strands are coated.
  3. Tin the pad with a small bump of solder.
  4. Join. Hold the tinned wire on the pad and touch the iron to both for 1–2 seconds until they flow together. Lift the iron and hold the wire still for 2 seconds.
  5. Inspect. A good joint is shiny and smooth, with a slight concave fillet. A dull, grainy ball is a cold joint: add flux and reheat. Solder linking two pads is a bridge: add flux and drag it off with the iron or wick.

Habits that save boards:

  • Heat for seconds, not tens of seconds. If it doesn't flow in 3 s, stop, add flux, and try again.
  • Feed solder to the joint, not to the iron tip.
  • The XIAO's edge pads are castellated (half-holes). Lay the wire along the pad face and solder into the half-hole. It's stronger than a wire standing on top.
  • Never let solder touch the XIAO's metal shield or reach the next pad.
  • Joints are electrical, not mechanical. Once wires are soldered, tack them down with Kapton or a dab of hot glue so the joint never carries the cable's weight. Paragliders vibrate.
  • Clean flux off with alcohol when done, and look again under magnification.
  • Touch something grounded before handling boards (static).

Build sequence

  1. Unpack, identify, take photos

    Lay out every part. Photograph both sides of the XIAO, the L76K and each Grove board. The silkscreen labels are tiny, and photos are easier to zoom into than the boards.

    Find the XIAO's BAT+ / BAT− pads on its underside (negative is the one nearest the USB port), and the L76K's pad names (3V3, GND, D0, D2, D6, D7).

  2. Separate the radio from the XIAO

    The Wio-SX1262 plugs into the XIAO's underside through a small board-to-board (B2B) connector. Unclip both antennas (lift one side of the U.FL first, never pull straight on the cable), then ease the Wio off. Lift evenly, rocking gently along the connector's length. Solder the XIAO without it: no heat near the connector, and the battery pads underneath are reachable.

    Don'tLeave D8, D9 and D10 unconnected. They carry the radio's SPI bus through the B2B connector.

  3. Make the distribution board

    Cut a piece of stripboard 4 copper strips wide and 7 holes long (about 10 × 18 mm), to fit the middle of the case. Strip 1 is 3V3, 2 is GND, 3 is SDA, 4 is SCL. Mark them with a pen on the plain side. Each strip takes one wire in from the XIAO and the rest out to the modules: 3V3 and GND carry five wires each, SDA and SCL three.

    CheckContinuity beep along each strip, and silence between neighbouring strips. Scrape any copper whiskers between strips with a knife.

  4. Prepare the Grove cables

    Each Grove cable has a white Grove plug on one end and four female jumper sockets on the other. Keep the Grove plug, cut off the jumper end, strip 2 mm and tin each wire.

    • BME280 and LSM6DS3: 6–8 cm long. All four wires go to the distribution board, colour to strip: red 3V3, black GND, white SDA, yellow SCL.
    • Buzzer: 12–15 cm, since it goes on the lid and needs slack to open it. Yellow is the signal, red is 3V3, black is GND. White is unused: cut it short and cover it with heat-shrink.
    Check coloursGrove plugs are keyed, but confirm the colour order against each module's silkscreen (SCL, SDA, VCC, GND) before soldering.

  5. Wire the XIAO

    Short wires (5–7 cm, 28 AWG or spare Grove wire) to the castellated pads:

    • 3V3, GND, D4, D5 → strips 3V3, GND, SDA, SCL on the distribution board.
    • D6, D7 → the L76K's D6 and D7 pads (step 6).
    • D0 → the buzzer cable's yellow wire. Join it with heat-shrink over the splice, or run it via a spare stripboard hole.
    CheckUnder magnification, no solder links neighbouring pads. Beep each wire end to its pad.

  6. Wire the L76K

    Don't solder its header sockets. Wire it beside the XIAO instead, so the XIAO's pads stay reachable. Match pad names exactly as if it were stacked: XIAO D6 → L76K D6, XIAO D7 → L76K D7. The module's own wiring handles which is TX and which is RX. Its 3V3 and GND come from the distribution board.

    Then the pull-ups: one 10 kΩ resistor from the module's D0 pad to its 3V3 pad, and another from D2 to 3V3. Seeed's FAQ says the module misbehaves if those two lines aren't held high. Bend the resistor legs flat along the board and sleeve them so they can't touch anything.

    Before powerPlug the GNSS patch antenna into the L76K's U.FL first. Seeed's guide says to attach it before use.

  7. Connect the modules to the distribution board

    Solder the BME280 and LSM6DS3 cables (4 wires each), the L76K's 3V3/GND pair, and the buzzer's red/black pair to their strips. Then plug the Grove plugs into the modules.

    CheckWith nothing powered, beep each net end to end: XIAO 3V3 to every module's VCC; GND to every GND; D4 to both SDA; D5 to both SCL; D6 to D6; D7 to D7; D0 to the buzzer SIG. Then check 3V3 to GND: the meter should not beep. The reading may climb as capacitors charge; a steady reading under ~10 Ω means a short somewhere.

  8. Battery leads and switch

    On the XIAO's underside, solder the battery-side socket lead: black to BAT− (the pad nearest the USB port), and red to BAT+ through the slide switch. Cut the red wire, solder each end to the switch (common pin plus one side), and heat-shrink the switch legs. Route the leads flat along the board, away from where the Wio sits.

    The full details and the polarity check are in the battery section below. Read it before plugging the battery in.

  9. Reassemble

    Clip the Wio-SX1262 back onto the B2B connector, pressing evenly until it seats. Refit the antennas: put one side of each U.FL on first, then press the other side down. Wi-Fi/BLE goes on the XIAO, LoRa on the Wio, and the GNSS patch on the L76K. U.FL connectors survive about 30 matings, so don't keep swapping them.

  10. First power: USB only, battery unplugged

    Plug USB-C into a computer. Measure the XIAO's 3V3 pad: about 3.3 V. Touch-test each board for 10 seconds; nothing should get warm. The red charge LED lights, then goes off after about 30 s with no battery (per Seeed).

    Now flashEverything is wired and checked, so this is the point your brief allows flashing: pio run -t upload, then pio device monitor. Run health and i2c, then follow docs/bringup.md from step 2.

  11. Battery in

    Switch off, confirm polarity at the plug (next section), plug the battery in, switch on. The unit should boot exactly as on USB. Unplug USB: still running means battery power works.

  12. Strain relief and a shake test

    Tack every wire bundle down with Kapton or small dabs of hot glue, near the joints but not on them. Then shake the board gently while watching the console: no resets and no sensor dropouts in health.

  13. Prepare the case

    Print the tray and the headless lid. Press the four M2 inserts into the corner bosses with the iron at about 220 °C, keeping them square. Test-fit the lid empty: the skirt slides in without force and the screws pull it flat. Push the foam into the pocket behind the vent hole.

  14. Into the case, in this order

    • Battery into its bay on foam tape, with Kapton over its top. Switch off, battery unplugged.
    • BME280 flat in front of it, sensor end against the foam.
    • XIAO stack into its cradle. Check the USB-C plug goes in through the hole before anything is taped.
    • L76K into its guides, and the slide switch into the end-wall window with a drop of glue from inside.
    • Distribution board flat in the middle; LSM6DS3 on foam tape on the battery.
    • In the lid: GNSS patch in its frame (ceramic side against the lid), buzzer in its frame, LoRa antenna taped at least 10 mm from the patch.
    CheckLid resting on, not screwed: switch on, run health. Everything still reports and nothing is pinched.

  15. Close up and test outside

    Screw the lid down. Outside with a clear sky, the GNSS should fix within a minute or two with the lid on. Run beep stay where you'll mount it. Tie the tether before the first flight.

The battery, properly

Connecting it

  • Use a cell with a protection board (a small PCB under the tape at the wire end). It cuts off on over-discharge, overcharge and short circuit. The XIAO charges the cell but doesn't protect it from those.
  • Polarity is the one mistake that kills boards. JST-PH leads are not standardised: some sellers wire them reversed. Before first connection, measure the battery plug with the meter. Then check that red on the battery side meets the wire going to BAT+.
  • Detachable, not soldered. A JST-PH socket on the XIAO side lets you pull the battery for storage or replacement without heat near the cell. Never solder directly to a LiPo's tabs.
  • Cut one wire at a time if you ever shorten battery leads. Cutting both at once with metal cutters shorts the cell.
  • The switch sits in the + lead, so it also controls charging. The charger feeds the BAT pads, so switch on to charge.

Charging and sizing

  • The XIAO charges at 100 mA. A 500 mAh cell takes about 5–6 h from empty, a 1000 mAh cell about 10–11 h: charge overnight. The red LED flashes while charging and goes out when full.
  • Estimated draw: 80–130 mA with Bluetooth to the XCTracer, GPS (about 41 mA alone), the radio listening and the vario beeping. That's an estimate: measure yours at bring-up with a USB power meter (battery unplugged).

Safety

  • Never short, crush, puncture or bend the cell. Wrap it in Kapton where it touches anything hard.
  • A swollen ("puffy") cell is finished. Retire it at a battery-recycling point.
  • Charge only between 0 and 45 °C: not in a hot car or cockpit in the sun, not below freezing. Stay nearby for the first few charges.
  • Store at half charge (about 3.8 V) if unused for weeks.
  • Flying commercially: a 1000 mAh cell is about 3.7 Wh, far under airline limits, but it must travel in cabin baggage, not in checked baggage.
